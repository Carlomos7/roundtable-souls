Roundtable Souls
================

A launcher for modded Elden Ring and Nightreign: it starts the game through
me3, keeps your Seamless Co-op settings in one place, manages your mods, and
checks and repairs your saves.


GETTING STARTED
---------------

You need Steam with Elden Ring or Nightreign, and me3
(https://github.com/garyttierney/me3). For Elden Ring, a Nightreign Revive
installation also works; it brings its own me3.

Windows, installer (recommended)
  Run RoundtableSouls-Setup.exe. It installs for your account only, with no
  administrator prompt, adds a Start menu entry, and can be removed from
  Settings > Apps like any other program.

Windows, portable
  Unzip RoundtableSouls.zip into a folder of its own and run
  RoundtableSouls.exe. It keeps its settings and logs in that folder.

  Either way, Windows may warn the first time because the program is not
  signed: choose "More info", then "Run anyway".

Linux and Steam Deck (Desktop Mode)
  Unpack RoundtableSouls-linux-x86_64.tar.gz and run RoundtableSouls. Install
  me3 for Linux first. The game runs through Proton; Roundtable Souls finds
  your saves inside the game's Proton folder, on the internal drive or an SD
  card.


GAMES
-----

The game button at the top of the window picks the game: click it for Elden
Ring, Nightreign, Dark Souls III or Sekiro, or press Ctrl+Tab and
Ctrl+Shift+Tab to step through them. Every page then works on that game: its
setups, its Seamless Co-op settings, its mods and its saves. Settings is shared
by all games. The window opens on the game you used last.

  Elden Ring    everything described below.
  Nightreign    Play, Co-op, Mods and Saves. Its Seamless Co-op has no
                password and three difficulty values. Nightreign encrypts its
                saves, so the Saves page checks that each file is whole and
                offers backups, restore, and copies between the co-op and
                standard save, but cannot show characters or repair them.
  Dark Souls III, Sekiro
                placeholders for now: their page shows whether the game, its
                saves and me3 profiles were found, and nothing on it can change
                them.

me3 profiles are listed under the game they were made for. The game cannot be
switched while a job is running.


THE PAGES
---------

Play
  Your character, the setup that Play will start, and the Play button. Leave
  the window open while you play; it can be minimised. After you quit it
  repairs your saves (see "Save repairs" below) and closes any leftover game
  processes. Setup lets you switch profiles or pick a .me3 file.

Co-op
  The Seamless Co-op session password, difficulty presets, and every other
  Seamless setting, each with a short explanation. Nothing is written until
  you press Save on the bar at the bottom (or Ctrl+S). Discard puts the page
  back to the file. Closing the window or switching games with unsaved changes
  asks: Save, Discard or Cancel.

  Difficulty presets are per extra player; only the host's numbers count:
    Seamless default         the mod's own values.
    Party of 3 (Nightreign)  enemies and bosses gain 100 % health per extra
                             player, poise rises a little, damage stays.
    Party of 4 / 5 / 6       the same total for a bigger group, in smaller
                             steps, so a full party is not overwhelming.
    Custom                   your own six numbers.

  Share with a friend puts all your Seamless settings into one block of text.
  Copy sends yours; Paste (or Open file...) takes a friend's, and Apply writes
  what differs after showing every change. Discard puts your own back.

Mods
  What the current me3 profile loads. Each mod can be turned on or off, has
  Options (load order and, for DLL mods, how they load) and can be removed.
  Removing takes the entry and its own comment lines only (the next entry's
  notes and section headings stay), and moves its folder to the Recycle Bin
  if you tick that. When the mod is inside combined parameters, you can
  Remove and rebuild in one go, so the game never keeps a removed mod's
  changes. Activity's Restore puts a removed mod back: the entry where it
  was, and the folder from the Recycle Bin. New profile and Delete profile
  manage .me3 files. Profile settings are me3's own options for this
  profile. Conflicts lists files two mods both replace and which one wins.

  Install mod takes a .zip, .7z or .rar, a .dll, or a folder; you can also
  drag one or more of them onto the Mods page and they install one after
  another. It finds the mod inside, even below a wrapper folder or next to a
  readme, and works out what kind it is. Before anything is copied you see:
    Which one        when the archive holds more than one (say an English
                     and an Italian version).
    Folder name, Id  what the mod's folder and its entry are called. The id
                     follows the name until you type your own.
    What to install  every file and folder, with why. Game files, the DLL and
                     its settings are ticked; readmes, an example .me3 (not
                     needed: the mod is added to your own profile) and other
                     mod loaders' DLLs are not. Files the launcher does not
                     recognise are installed as shipped. Tick or untick any.
    regulation.bin   the game's parameters. me3 uses only one: the last
                     package in the load order that has one. When another
                     package already has one (Nightreign Revive, say), the
                     new mod goes before it by default, so that one keeps
                     working; pick Last to use the new mod's instead, or
                     untick regulation.bin to leave it out.
  New mods otherwise go at the end of the load order; Options changes it,
  and anything else, once the mod is in.

  Combined parameters (Elden Ring). me3 uses one regulation.bin (the game's
  parameters: weapons, enemies, items...): the last one in the load order.
  When several packs ship one, the launcher can combine them into one file
  so all of them apply. It compares each pack with the game's own
  regulation.bin and applies only what each pack changed, row by row, in
  load order: two packs that change different parts of the same row both
  apply; where they change the same values, the later pack wins, and the
  log lists those rows. Rows a pack lacks are kept, and a table made for
  another game version is left out for that pack (the log says so). The
  result is a package the launcher manages, combined-parameters, placed
  after the packs it combines.

  Some packages that must stay last come with a rebuild tool of their own
  that folds the regulation.bin (and files such as the talk file behind the
  menus at graces) of the package before them into their copy. The launcher
  finds one from the files next to that package: a rebuild.json describing
  the tool (docs/Rebuild tools.md in the project explains it), or a record
  of what it merged, with checksums, beside its setup files. It asks once
  before a tool runs for the first time (and again when the tool changes).
  With such a package, combined-parameters goes right before it, so its
  tool takes the combined file and every pack still applies.
    Install          a pack with a regulation.bin offers, ticked, to combine
                     it with the other packs and / or run the rebuild tool
                     after the install. Its parameters apply once that
                     succeeds.
    Mods page        a pill next to the profile name: green (Parameters OK)
                     or red (out of date, only one of several packs applies,
                     or the last rebuild failed). Click it for the Load order
                     card, which says why and has Combine (several packs,
                     only one applies) or Rebuild (a pack was turned on or
                     off, reordered, removed or updated, or the game
                     updated; the game must be closed).
    Rebuild          the profile is saved as a .bak first. Rebuild tools
                     usually rewrite the profile without comments; when it
                     still loads the same things, your own text comes back
                     with only the tool's own load order lists updated. The
                     result is checked against what went in: a rebuild that
                     did not take each file from the right pack is reported
                     as failed, never as done.
    Play             when a merge (combined parameters, or a mod's own
                     rebuild tool) is out of date because mods, their load
                     order or the game changed, Play rebuilds it first and
                     then starts the game; Cancel Play lets the rebuild
                     finish and does not start. A failed rebuild keeps the
                     previous result and offers Play anyway (not tried
                     again with the same mods until you restart the
                     launcher). After an automatic rebuild only the newest
                     3 of the tool's backups stay; older ones go to the
                     Recycle Bin. Turn it off with Update merged mods
                     before Play on Settings; Play then only warns. It
                     never makes a combined package by itself: when only
                     one of several packs applies, Play warns. Play from a
                     Steam shortcut does the same when the tool was
                     already allowed, and starts anyway if it fails.
  Which files a pack has is always read from the pack itself; nothing asks
  you to label a mod. If the launcher does not recognise a package that
  must stay last (a fork, or a newer version that names its files
  differently), turn on Parameter overlay in that package's Options. Its
  rebuild tool is then looked for with looser rules, or you can pick a
  rebuild.json for it there; installs place new packs before it even when no
  tool is found. That choice, and the mods you keep after it on purpose, are
  kept in roundtable.json next to the profile, so they go with the folder
  when you copy it to another PC.
  When the package that must stay last is there but its setup files are not
  (copied without its hidden setup folder, say), Load order names what is
  missing, and Rebuild stops before changing anything. Run the installer it
  came with again, or put the files back.

  Merging files two mods ship. When two packages ship the same game file,
  me3 uses only the later one's copy. For the files the launcher can merge
  (archives such as menu layouts, animations and effects, and text), a
  Combine or Rebuild merges them instead: it reads the game's own copy from
  its archives, sees what each mod changed, added or removed inside, and
  puts all of it into one file in the combined package, after the mods it
  merges. Where two mods change the same part (one texture, one text
  entry), the later mod's is used and the log says so. Load order then
  shows those copies as combined; a changed copy or a game update shows the
  merge as out of date, and Play updates it first. Other files are left as
  they are (the later mod's copy is used). Oodle-compressed files, which is
  most of Elden Ring's, need the game's own Oodle library, so merging them
  works on Windows.

  Build in the launcher (preview, on Settings). For a mod the launcher has
  a recipe for (Nightreign Revive LITE 0.1.33), a rebuild merges it from
  the download kept in its setup folder instead of running its installer.
  The launcher merges its animations, effects, menu text and parameters
  itself, the same way as above; only the grace menu still goes through
  the mod's own tool (two mods changing the same menu script need that
  tool). The content is the same as the installer's (checked on a real
  profile). Your profile is never rewritten, RevivePrototype.ini keeps
  your values (a new version only adds its new settings), and one previous
  build is kept in .roundtable-build for Undo rebuild, instead of a full
  backup on every run. Other versions, or with the switch off, run the
  mod's installer as before.

  Stays last. The package that must stay last (and its DLL) loads after
  every other mod, however mods are added: installing, adding existing
  folders, removing, renaming an id and saving options keep its own
  load_after lists naming every other package and DLL, each optional, on or
  off, so switching a mod on or off never rewrites them. Only its own
  entries are changed; yours stay as you wrote them. New mods go right
  before it in the file. The install dialog says so and has an advanced
  option to load a mod after it instead (only for a mod made to go on top
  of it: its files replace the merged ones). A mod that names it in its own
  Load after is kept after it too. When an edit by hand leaves a mod after
  it, Load order says so, with Fix order (put it last again) and Keep it
  after (on purpose; Put them before undoes that). A change that would make
  the load order loop is not made, and Load order says why.

  Undo. A copy of the profile is kept before every change the launcher
  makes (the newest 30, in the data folder). Switching a mod, its options or
  a profile setting shows Undo right after; Edit profile > Versions lists
  every copy with why it was made and restores any of them. A rebuild keeps
  what it replaced: the launcher's combined parameters (the last 3) and the
  rebuild tool's own backup. Undo rebuild on Activity swaps them back by
  renaming, so it takes no time or space, and Redo swaps them again.
  Rebuild tools keep a backup every run and never remove them; Load order
  shows how much space they use and can move all but the newest 3 to the
  Recycle Bin.

  Load order (on the Mods page) says what this profile loads and what wins:
    Stays last       whether the package that must stay last loads after
                     every other mod, with Fix order and Keep it after when
                     one loads after it.
    Parameters       the pill's status with its reasons, Combine or
                     Rebuild, and the rows two packs both changed in the
                     last combine (the later pack won those).
    Files            every file two packages both ship, and what happens to
                     the earlier copy: replaced (me3 uses the later one's),
                     combined (a rebuild or the combine was built from it, as
                     it is now), combined but out of date (rebuild to bring
                     it in), or does not reach the rebuild (another pack's
                     copy sits in between; the rebuild takes the nearest).
    Problems         entries me3 would refuse: a missing folder, a required
                     load-after that is missing or off, a loop.
  It opens by itself when the pill turns red.

  Each package and DLL has a tag naming the folder it sits in (SeamlessCoop
  for ersc.dll); hover it for the full path. The packages folder (mod) and
  the natives folder show at the top of their lists when something there
  needs saying: an entry that points at the folder itself (it only holds the
  mods listed below, so it loads nothing and can be removed), and Not loaded,
  which lists the mod folders and DLLs in there that no entry points at and
  adds the ones you tick. Entries that me3 would refuse (a missing file, a
  required load-after that is missing or off, a load order that loops) say so
  in red.

  A DLL mod's settings button (the page icon on its row) opens its own
  settings file, for example UnlockTheFps.ini, to edit and save. Files beside
  the DLL are found on their own; if a mod reads a file under another name,
  Attach a file points at it once and it is remembered. Seamless Co-op's settings
  are on the Co-op page.

  Edit profile shows the .me3 text, with Save and Discard under it (Discard
  reads the file again):
    Ctrl+F find, Ctrl+H replace (Aa matches case, .* uses patterns),
    Enter and Shift+Enter step through matches, Esc closes the search bar,
    Ctrl+/ comments lines in or out, Tab and Shift+Tab indent a selection,
    Ctrl+D duplicates a line, Ctrl+Z undoes any of these in one step.

Saves
  Every save file with its characters and a few status notes:
    Loads fine / May not load   whether something would hang the loading
                                screen.
    Mod items                   items the game itself does not define.
    Opens in save editors       whether save editors can read it.

  Review & fix opens one save on its own page. Every possible fix is a tick
  box: loading fixes and checksum fixes start ticked, removals start off.
  Apply shows what will change, makes a backup, writes, checks the result,
  and offers Undo.

    May not load        Torrent stuck at 0 HP, a character standing nowhere,
                        or in the Land of Shadow on a PC without the DLC.
                        The fix marks Torrent as fallen, or moves the
                        character to Roundtable Hold.
    Remove mod items    takes items the game does not define off a character.
                        They are named from the mod that added them (for
                        example Seamless Co-op's "Tiny Great Pot"); when a
                        mod's text cannot be read, by kind and ID, such as
                        "Armour 742000". Worn items have to come off in-game
                        first. Use this before copying a co-op character to
                        a standard save.
    For save editors    recomputes stale checksums and repairs the block
                        me3 leaves behind.

  The line at the top says which file Play uses and which one Play offline
  uses. That follows your setup: a save file name set in the me3 profile, and
  Seamless Co-op's own save file setting, are both honoured, so a renamed
  save is listed and repaired after play like the default one.

  Every save shows the characters it would have before and after any copy:
    Copy to...           copies the whole file over another save (the co-op
                         save over the standard one, say). The save it
                         replaces is kept in the save library first, under a
                         name you choose. Or add the copy to the library only,
                         and nothing live changes.
    Copy a character...  copies one character into a free slot, or over one,
                         in the same or another save. Characters from another
                         Steam account are moved to this one. A co-op
                         character keeps Seamless Co-op's items; Remove mod
                         items takes them off for a standard save.
    Add to library...    keeps a named copy of the whole file.
  Copy report puts a plain-text summary on the clipboard.

  The save library keeps named copies of whole save files. Swap in puts one in
  place of a live save: the save it replaces goes into the library first (you
  name it; the default says who is in it and when), so nothing is ever lost,
  and Undo works as for any change. Import a save file adds one from anywhere.
  library.json records every copy and every swap, rename and removal. Removing
  a copy moves its file to the library's removed folder. Library copies are
  never removed for you.

  Backups lists the backup taken before every change to a save, named for
  what came next ("Before fixing checksums", "Before playing"). The newest 20
  of each save and everything from the last 7 days are kept; Keep (the pin)
  holds on to one for good. Restore puts a backup back, and backs up the save
  as it is then, so a restore can be undone too.

  What counts as a game item comes from the installed game itself, including
  Shadow of the Erdtree and the Tarnished Edition pack's gear, so official
  items are never listed as mod items. Shadow of the Erdtree is detected from
  the game folder and the Tarnished Edition pack from your save; nothing to
  set.

  A save damaged by a crash while saving is named as such and left alone:
  restore a backup from before the crash.

Settings
  Launcher: the version and whether this copy is installed or portable,
  Check for updates, the releases page and the launcher's logs.
  Steam shortcut: see "Playing from Steam" below.
  Appearance: dark or light, and the logo.
  Maintenance: repair saves now, and clear leftover game processes (Windows).
  Play session: turn each step around the game on or off.
  Locations: point at a me3, game or profile folder that is not where it
  usually is. Blank means detect.
  me3: its version and folders, and a notice when a newer me3 is out.
  Offline play: start the game without Seamless Co-op when Steam cannot sign
  in (co-op itself needs Steam online).

  Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5 open the pages. Nothing has keyboard focus when the window
  opens, so a stray key press cannot start the game.


PLAYING FROM STEAM (Big Picture, Steam Deck Gaming Mode)
--------------------------------------------------------

Roundtable Souls can run Play without its window:

  1. In Steam, choose Add a Game > Add a Non-Steam Game and pick
     RoundtableSouls (RoundtableSouls.exe on Windows).
  2. In that shortcut's Properties, set Launch Options to the game:
       --game er --play      Elden Ring
       --game nr --play      Nightreign
     Settings > Steam shortcut shows the exact target and options for the
     game picked at the top, with Copy buttons. Make one shortcut per game.

Starting the shortcut uses the setup Play last used for that game, launches
it, and (Elden Ring) repairs your saves when you quit, just like the Play
button. Without --game it plays the game the window showed last. Open the
window once in Desktop Mode first so a setup is remembered.


UPDATES
-------

Roundtable Souls checks for a new version at most once an hour when it starts
(Settings > Launcher can turn that off, or check right away). When one is out,
a bar offers Update now (or Download) and Skip this version. It downloads the new version, checks it against the
published checksums, and restarts into it. Your settings, logs and backups are
kept. Nothing is ever installed without that click. Skip this version hides that one version.


SAVE REPAIRS
------------

Playing through me3 leaves a block at the end of the save that save editors
cannot read. The game does not mind. After every session, Roundtable Souls
puts the game's own data back into that block. Every rewritten save is backed
up first (see Backups on the Saves page), and nothing else in it is touched.


WHERE FILES LIVE
----------------

Everything Roundtable Souls keeps is in one folder, its data folder:
  installed on Windows   %LOCALAPPDATA%\RoundtableSouls
  portable               next to the program (or the folder above when that
                         folder cannot be written)
  Linux                  ~/.local/share/RoundtableSouls when the program's
                         folder cannot be written

Inside it:
  launcher_settings.json                 your settings
  logs\                                  what happened, for when something goes wrong
  cache\                                 checksums of large mod files, so checks stay quick
  saves\<game>\<Steam account>\backups\  a backup before every change to a save
  saves\<game>\<Steam account>\library\  the save library (library.json, copies, removed\)
  profiles\deleted\                      .me3 files deleted on the Mods page
  temp\                                  mods being unpacked while they install

Nothing of Roundtable Souls' goes into the game's save folder or me3's profile
folders, apart from three things that must sit beside their file: the
.offline.me3 copy Play offline uses (me3 reads a profile's paths from its
folder), one .bak next to a .me3 or settings file you edited, and a moment's
temporary file while a save is written. Backups and deleted profiles from
earlier versions are moved into the data folder automatically.

Activity (in the sidebar) lists what the launcher did: every Play, repair,
install and rebuild, grouped by day, with how it went (Done, Done with
warnings, Failed, Stopped, or Interrupted when the launcher closed first) and
what it did. Open one for its log; Show details adds the fine detail, and a
Play also keeps me3's own output. Filter by game, kind or failures. A red count
on Activity says how many jobs failed since you last looked. Copy for support
and Save logs for support mask your user name and Steam IDs; nothing is sent
anywhere.

If something goes wrong, the logs folder says what happened (Logs folder on
Activity, or under Log on the Play page, opens it):
  jobs\<date>_<what>.log    one file per job (Play, a repair, an install, a
                            rebuild...) with every step, and how it ended
  jobs\<...>.me3.log        me3's own output for that Play
  jobs.jsonl                what each job was, when, how long and how it ended
  launcher.log              everything else worth keeping, and any crash with
                            its details (the newest 2 MB and five older files)
  old\                      logs from earlier versions, kept for 30 days
Jobs and their files are kept for 14 days, or the newest 50, whichever keeps
more. Nothing is sent anywhere.
